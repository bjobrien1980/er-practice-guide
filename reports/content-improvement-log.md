# ER Practice Guide — Content Improvement Log

**Purpose:** Running log of recommendations arising from critical review of the live guide.

**Scope reviewed:** 15 live modules at `http://localhost:4321`.

**Initial review recorded:** 2026-11-12 11:38 AWST

**Status:** Recommendations only. No guide content has been changed by this log.

## Design principle

The guide should distinguish clearly between:

1. **Legal baseline** — legislation, binding authority and applicable legal rules.
2. **Context-dependent legal assessment** — questions whose answer depends on the facts, applicable award/enterprise agreement/contract/policy or procedural setting.
3. **Local or optional practice** — an employer's own process, delegation, template, risk control or preferred operating model.

The core guide should teach the legal questions, source checks and consequences. It should not present one employer's ER process as a universal requirement. Any optional workflow should be labelled as an example or moved into a local overlay.

## Recommendations

### R-001 — Add module-level version control and update timestamp

**Priority:** High

Every module should display explicit content-control metadata, including:

- module version;
- last updated timestamp;
- last legal/source verification timestamp, if different;
- reviewer or review status;
- relevant legislation/case-law currency status.

At minimum, each module should carry a visible `Version` and `Last updated` field. The guide should distinguish an editorial update from a legal verification. `Last updated` must not be treated as confirmation that the law was last checked at that time.

### R-002 — Replace the current draft metadata with a meaningful status model

**Priority:** High

The current `Draft: pending legal review / Last verified: not yet / Reviewer: unassigned` presentation is transparent but too blunt for ongoing use. Define separate statuses for:

- content drafting;
- source/citation verification;
- legal review;
- operational/local-overlay review;
- publication readiness.

### R-003 — Add a portable “Start here” orientation

**Priority:** High

Add a short introductory module that explains how to frame a new matter without prescribing a universal employer workflow. It should ask:

- What is the presenting issue?
- Is it conduct, performance, capacity, attendance, redundancy or a mixed matter?
- What legal and contractual instruments may apply?
- Are there immediate factual, safety, welfare or evidence issues?
- What decisions have and have not yet been made?
- What facts remain uncertain?
- Does the matter require escalation under the organisation's own arrangements?

This should be framed as a decision map, not a mandatory process.

### R-004 — Add an integrated pathway map

**Priority:** High

Show how a matter may engage more than one pathway:

- unfair dismissal;
- general protections;
- industrial activity;
- discrimination law;
- award, enterprise agreement or contractual procedure;
- capacity/medical issues;
- safety or psychosocial risk;
- genuine redundancy;
- notice and summary dismissal.

The map should identify the legal source to check, without implying that every matter follows the same route.

### R-005 — Reconsider the primary learning sequence

**Priority:** Medium

The current structure is principally a s.387/unfair-dismissal reference sequence. Consider retaining that structure as a legal-reference path while adding a separate practical learning path based on the life of a matter:

- framing the issue;
- identifying the applicable sources;
- investigating and testing facts;
- notification and response;
- decision-making;
- termination consequences;
- records and escalation;
- Commission/remedy consequences.

The alternative path should be explanatory rather than a universal mandatory procedure.

### R-006 — Add a glossary and plain-language definitions

**Priority:** High

Define on first use, and make searchable:

- adverse action;
- workplace right;
- reverse onus;
- minimum employment period;
- high-income threshold;
- genuine redundancy;
- serious misconduct;
- summary dismissal;
- show cause;
- support person;
- representation;
- Briginshaw considerations;
- reinstatement;
- mitigation;
- repudiation;
- PIP, EA, FWA and FWC.

### R-007 — Reframe checklists as legal/context tests, not universal process instructions

**Priority:** High

Checklist items should ask whether a legal or factual question has been considered and what source controls it. They should not require a particular workflow unless the requirement is statutory or arises from an identified instrument.

For example, prefer:

> “I have checked whether the employee requested a support person and whether an applicable agreement, policy or established process governs the opportunity to have one.”

over:

> “I have offered the employee a support person.”

Optional templates and workflows should be labelled as examples and should direct the user to check local arrangements.

### R-008 — Add an explicit local-overlay boundary

**Priority:** High

Create a clearly marked section explaining that local arrangements may control:

- delegations;
- approval requirements;
- investigation roles;
- response periods;
- templates;
- record systems;
- escalation pathways;
- consultation obligations;
- enterprise-specific terminology.

The portable legal guide should not silently convert these into universal practice rules.

### R-009 — Complete the missing Tier 3 situation modules

**Priority:** High

The following modules are currently marked “coming soon” and represent central conduct/performance situations:

- safety breaches;
- bullying and harassment;
- medical capacity;
- drug and alcohol;
- dishonesty and falsified records;
- absenteeism and abandonment;
- social media and out-of-hours conduct.

Prioritise according to the intended audience and operating context, but do not present any organisation-specific response model as generally required.

### R-010 — Expand performance content beyond dismissal defensibility

**Priority:** High

The current performance-warning material is primarily about s.387(e). Add the legal and analytical distinctions needed to assess:

- expected standard;
- actual performance;
- role clarity;
- support, training and resources;
- capacity versus conduct;
- medical or disability issues;
- reasonable adjustment questions;
- warnings and future consequences;
- whether the issue is genuinely performance-related.

These should be framed as matters to analyse, not as a prescribed PIP model.

### R-011 — Make investigation guidance method-neutral but analytically useful

**Priority:** Medium

The investigation module has strong principles but should explain the questions that determine adequacy:

- What exactly is alleged?
- What evidence could establish or contradict it?
- What source or instrument governs the investigation?
- Was the process sufficiently impartial for the decision being made?
- Were obvious contrary sources considered?
- Could the employee understand and answer the case?
- Are findings separated from recommendations?

Avoid presenting one mandatory sequence of interviews or one investigation template as legally required.

### R-012 — Add privacy, confidentiality and record-handling boundaries

**Priority:** High

Clarify the legal and contextual limits around:

- need-to-know access;
- medical information;
- confidentiality directions;
- privilege and non-privileged ER records;
- witness communications;
- promises of confidentiality;
- retention and discoverability.

Identify which issues are legal questions and which depend on local policy or information-governance arrangements.

### R-013 — Revisit the “four gateways in the right order” formulation

**Priority:** High

Present the four topics as practical screening questions, not necessarily as a rigid universal sequence. Explain precisely how each answer affects the matter and distinguish:

- protection from unfair dismissal;
- the small-business pathway;
- genuine redundancy;
- the ordinary s.387 merits assessment.

Avoid wording such as “a yes to the wrong one ends the case” unless the exact legal consequence is stated.

### R-014 — Reframe general protections “four doors”

**Priority:** High

The reverse onus should not be presented as a substantive protected category alongside workplace rights, industrial activity and discrimination. Describe it as an evidentiary rule that operates once the relevant statutory facts and adverse action are established. Make clear that the selected pathways are illustrative rather than exhaustive of Part 3-1.

### R-015 — Qualify the support-person guidance

**Priority:** High

The proposition that s.387(d) does not impose a positive obligation to offer a support person unprompted should be accompanied immediately by the qualification that an award, enterprise agreement, policy, contract or established process may impose a broader obligation. Avoid allowing the legal distinction to be read as a universal operational recommendation.

### R-016 — Qualify the suspension guidance

**Priority:** High

The statement that suspension pending investigation is “routine and low-risk” is too broad. Even paid suspension can raise contractual, procedural, psychosocial, industrial and prejudgment issues. Explain that the legal basis and risk depend on the contract, applicable instrument, policy, facts and purpose of the suspension.

### R-017 — Qualify conciliation and settlement propositions

**Priority:** Medium

The Commission-proceedings module should qualify:

- “without prejudice” treatment;
- confidentiality;
- the circumstances in which an oral agreement may be binding;
- settlement authority;
- the value of prompt written confirmation;
- the difference between a conciliation outcome and a formal order.

### R-018 — Add authority-type labels to propositions

**Priority:** High

For important propositions, distinguish visibly between:

- statutory text;
- binding authority;
- Full Bench authority;
- single-member FWC authority;
- secondary or explanatory material;
- a synthesis or inference from the corpus.

The current case tables are useful but do not always make that distinction clear to a novice.

### R-019 — Fix incomplete sentences and missing source-linked text

**Priority:** Critical

The live pages contain visible sentence fragments and apparently lost text around citations. Examples include incomplete propositions in the small-business Code, costs, policy-incorporation and general-protections modules. These must be repaired before wider use.

### R-020 — Remove duplicated citations and run rendered-content QA

**Priority:** High

At least one module displays a duplicated citation, such as:

> `[2022] FWC 2033 at [17] [2022] FWC 2033 at [17]`

Add a rendered-content QA check that detects:

- duplicated citations;
- citation-only sentence fragments;
- missing linked text;
- unfinished clauses;
- broken authority tables;
- inconsistent section references;
- content that differs between source markdown and rendered page.

### R-021 — Improve source and case-table transparency

**Priority:** Medium

For each important case or proposition, show enough information for a novice to understand:

- what proposition the case supports;
- whether the proposition is a holding or observation;
- whether the authority was independently read;
- whether an appeal or later authority affects its weight;
- whether the case is illustrative rather than controlling.

Retain the existing distinction between directly confirmed and indirectly quoted authorities, but surface it closer to the relevant proposition.

### R-022 — Add a current-law and review warning

**Priority:** High

The guide should explain that case law, thresholds, regulations, Commission procedure and statutory provisions may change. Where a proposition depends on a current figure, regulation or procedural rule, show the relevant currency date and source-check status.

### R-023 — Maintain separate content and legal-review logs

**Priority:** Medium

This improvement log should record editorial and pedagogical recommendations. A separate legal-review register should record:

- proposition reviewed;
- source checked;
- date checked;
- reviewer;
- outcome;
- required qualification;
- unresolved issue.

This avoids treating an editorial review as legal approval.

### R-024 — Preserve the guide’s strongest existing features

**Priority:** Ongoing

Retain:

- plain-language opening propositions;
- “What you’ll be able to do” sections;
- separation of legal tests that are often conflated;
- failure patterns;
- worked scenarios;
- checklists, once reframed as context-sensitive tests;
- explicit source and corpus limitations;
- emphasis on the actual decision-maker and evidence;
- escalation as a judgment issue rather than an automatic process rule.

## Review rule for future entries

Before adding a recommendation, identify whether it is:

- a legal requirement;
- a context-dependent legal question;
- a source/provenance issue;
- a pedagogical improvement; or
- an optional/local practice suggestion.

If it is optional or local, do not place it in the core guide as if it were universal.

### L-004 — Fragile or non-canonical decision URLs require normalisation

**Priority:** High

Several authority links do not contain the cited decision number in the URL slug, or use generic/document identifiers. Examples that were reachable in Chrome but remain difficult to audit include:

- `[2021] FWC 3903` → slug ending `2021-fwc`;
- `[2025] FWC 777` → slug ending in `2025`;
- `[2023] FWC 991` → slug ending `379304`.

These may be valid FWC routing slugs, so they should not be called broken without a successful document lookup. They are nevertheless difficult to audit and vulnerable to search/index changes. Build a canonical authority registry containing the citation, case name, verified official URL or PDF, source type and last checked timestamp, then generate module links from that registry. The links already identified as loading the wrong document are recorded separately under L-003.

### L-005 — Link QA should verify identity, not only HTTP status

**Priority:** High

A future automated check should test each authority link for:

- HTTP reachability where the source permits automated access;
- final URL after redirects;
- document title and citation;
- case name against the citation displayed in the module;
- correct paragraph/pinpoint range where the link claims to support one;
- source authority type;
- last verification timestamp.

A link that returns HTTP 200 but opens the wrong decision should be treated as an accuracy failure. Conversely, a link returning 403 from an anti-bot edge should be recorded as “not verified”, not automatically as broken.

### L-006 — Add link-status metadata to the source model

**Priority:** Medium

Each external authority should have a recorded status such as:

- verified live;
- verified via official PDF;
- redirect normalised;
- temporarily inaccessible;
- citation/identity mismatch;
- unresolved.

The rendered module should not need to display all of this, but the build and legal-review records should retain it alongside the module version and last-update timestamp.
